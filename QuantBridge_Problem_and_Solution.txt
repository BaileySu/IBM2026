Small investment teams often build a portfolio risk view and a separate research table from the same holdings and prices. Each new tool can force an analyst and a developer to settle the same questions again. Which positions count toward a fund's equity value? Which prices support a volatility calculation? A disagreement can leave two credible-looking reports with different answers.

QuantBridge lets a researcher describe the question in ordinary language. The assistant clarifies ambiguous requests and looks up the approved definitions and available price history. Local calculations supply the numbers. A risk page and an independent research export use the same holdings, trading calendar and adjusted prices.

The prototype uses real public holdings for IVV and OEF and daily prices for ten stocks. On the October 21, 2026 snapshot, the five largest positions accounted for 28.28% of IVV's equity value and 38.27% of OEF's. Changing the window from 20 to 60 trading sessions moved NVDA's annualized volatility from 25.01% to 35.79%. Adding JPM exposed a different issue. Both funds held JPM, but the snapshot lacked its price history. QuantBridge displayed the positions, left the affected metrics blank and asked whether to save a partial result or wait.

Each confirmed result records its inputs, version and coverage decision. The earlier 20-session result remained available after the change. When we tested a holdings file with renamed columns and amounts expressed in cents, all 613 position values matched and both tools reproduced their results. These checks give a small team a way to reuse a calculation and verify that its meaning survives the next change.
